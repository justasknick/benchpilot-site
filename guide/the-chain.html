How US IT staffing works: the chain

4 min read · Updated 23 October 2026

Almost every requirement you see on LinkedIn sits somewhere in a chain of companies. Knowing where you are in that chain tells you who to talk to, how much money is in the role, and why one consultant must never reach the same client twice.

The usual chain

  1. End client The company where the consultant will actually work: a bank, a retailer, a hospital.
  2. Implementation partner A large services firm running the project for the client. Not every role has one.
  3. Prime vendor A staffing firm with a direct contract with the client (or the client's managed service provider). It receives the requirement first.
  4. Vendor or sub-vendor Staffing firms that the prime shares the requirement with. Most LinkedIn posts come from here.
  5. Your company The employer of your consultant. On a C2C deal you invoice the vendor above you.
  6. Your consultant Paid by your company, usually on your W2 payroll or as an H-1B employee.

How the money flows

The end client pays the top of the chain an hourly bill rate. Each layer keeps a margin and pays the layer below. By the time the rate reaches you, two or three margins may have been taken. That is why the same role can show $90/hr in one post and $65/hr in another.

A shorter chain means a better rate and faster answers. A post that names the end client and comes from a prime vendor is usually worth more of your time than one that is four layers away.

Why the chain matters for submissions

Several vendors often carry the same requirement. If you send one consultant to the same end client through two vendors, the client sees the same résumé twice and usually rejects both.
