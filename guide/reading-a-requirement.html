Reading a requirement post

5 min read · Updated 23 October 2026

A requirement post is short, but every line changes who you can submit. Read it in this order before you think about a consultant.

1. Role and must-have skills

The title ("Sr. Java Developer") and the skills list. Separate the must-haves (often written as "must have", "mandatory", or listed first) from the nice-to-haves. Years of experience are usually firm.

2. Location and work mode

  • Onsite: in the office every day. Hybrid: some days a week ("3 days onsite"). Remote: from anywhere in the US unless it says otherwise.
  • "Locals only": the consultant must already live within commuting distance. Relocating after the offer usually doesn't count.
  • Some posts ask for a driving licence or ID with a local address as proof.

3. Duration and type

"6+ months", "12 months, extendable", "contract to hire" (the client may hire the consultant full time later). Longer projects are worth more effort.

4. Rate and tax terms

  • C2C (corp-to-corp): your company invoices the vendor; the consultant stays your employee. Most bench sales work is C2C.
  • W2: the consultant goes on the vendor's payroll. A "W2 only" post usually means you cannot submit through your company.
  • 1099: the consultant is an independent contractor paid directly.

"$65/hr C2C" is what the vendor will pay your company per hour. "DOE" means "depends on experience": ask.

5. Visa lines

The post says which work authorisations the client accepts. Common ones:

  • USC US citizen. GC green card (permanent resident).
  • GC-EAD, H4-EAD, L2-EAD employment authorisation documents that allow work for any employer.
  • H-1B needs an employer to sponsor; moving to a new employer needs a transfer.
  • OPT / CPT students working on their study visa's training permission.
  • "Independent visas" or "no H-1B" usually means USC, GC and EADs only.

Immigration rules change. Check your company's own immigration process before you promise anything about a consultant's status.

6. Client, job ID and the recruiter

A named client and a job ID make a requirement real and let you check for repeat submissions. The recruiter's email and phone at the bottom are your contact; save them.
