Right to represent (RTR)

3 min read · Updated 23 October 2026

A right to represent (RTR) is a short written confirmation that a vendor may submit your consultant to one specific client and job, for a set time. Vendors ask for it before they submit, so that two vendors don't send the same person.

What it usually contains

  • The consultant's name, the role, the end client and, if known, the job ID.
  • The rate agreed with the vendor.
  • That no other firm will submit the consultant to that client for this role (often for 30 to 90 days).
  • The consultant's or your company's confirmation, often by email reply.

Why it matters to you

If the same consultant reaches the client twice, through two vendors, the client often rejects the consultant and may stop working with both vendors. One RTR per client per role keeps your consultant safe and your vendors happy.

Good habits

  • Get the consultant's consent for each role before you give an RTR.
  • Keep every RTR (the email is enough) and note where each consultant went.
  • If a second vendor sends the same role, tell them the consultant is already represented.
